# Reviewer brief — *Actor-Indexed Determination*

*Companion to the paper. One page. The first brief in this series was written for an information
theorist, and asked whether an identification was sound. This one is written for the framework's
natural critic: someone who thinks task difficulty is actor-relative all the way down, or that
governance vocabulary cannot be made precise enough to carry weight.*

---

## What the paper claims

That unresolved determination is indexed by a tuple — task, ground, acceptance relation, tolerance,
arrangement, assurance — rather than by the task alone; that behavioural commitments attach at three
composing levels; that a resolution's source and its assurance mechanism are separate dimensions;
that every completed act's determination demand is discharged by one of four modes, so **demand is
never unmet, only ungoverned**; and that on the region where the acceptance predicate closes, that
demand is measurable and its conservation is the chain rule of entropy.

**Almost all of this is `projected`** — proposed with a declared falsifier and not yet met. The
paper says so in its own §1, not only in its appendix, and Appendix A shows every cited node's
status straight from the graph.

## What it deliberately does not claim

- **No empirical result.** The five hypotheses are predictions. Their evidence fields are empty, and
  the appendix renders them empty so a reader cannot miss it.
- **No human/model boundary.** The framework predicts how comparative advantage *shifts* with
  evaluability, ground access, feedback and accountability. It does not name a frontier.
- **No general conservation invariant.** Conservation is a **principle**; it is a measured identity
  only where the predicate closes, and the paper states the bound rather than implying it.
- **No claim that a closed predicate makes anything cheap.** The framework retired that claim and
  the paper carries the retirement, in §10.

## Where the falsifiers live

In the graph, attached to the nodes, not composed for the paper. H1 fails if comparative advantage
does not track evaluability at matched difficulty and resources. H3 fails if generator-plus-checker
does not beat both baselines inside its declared region. H5 fails if selection reliance does not
track evaluation speed, objectivity, stationarity and completeness at matched labour supply,
training cost, consequence and task structure. The discharge claim fails on an act exhibiting a
fifth supply mode, or an act completing with an outcome-relevant alternative resolved by nothing at
all.

**The paper's own falsifier, stated in §9.5:** not a single failed prediction, but a pattern in
which arrangement-level variables carry no more predictive weight than executor kind.

## The strongest objection, and where it is pre-answered

**The objection.** *"Actor-indexed determination is 'it depends' in a lab coat. If irreducibility is
a property of a six-place relation, every counterexample is absorbed by adjusting a coordinate. The
framework cannot be wrong, so it says nothing."*

This is the objection the paper expects, and it is the right one to press. Four places answer it,
and a reviewer should test each rather than take them on trust.

1. **The framework locates the floor in the predicate, not in the relativity.** *The intrinsic floor
   is a property of the acceptance predicate, not of the decision* [`term:floor`, settled]. That is
   a commitment, and it is refutable: exhibit a task whose floor moves while its acceptance predicate
   and declared ground stay fixed. §6.1.
2. **The measured region has a hard boundary drawn by an `iff`, not by convenience.** The measure
   exists if and only if the acceptance predicate operationally closes [`DDD-measure-06`,
   established]. A framework tuning its own domain to survive would not fix that domain by a
   biconditional it cannot move. §4.4, §6.1.
3. **One separation is established and non-relative.** Closure is distinct from generation cost
   [`DDD-frame-06`, established] — verification being cheap implies nothing about the acceptance
   region. This is not indexed to anything, and a counterexample would be a real refutation. §5.3.
4. **The framework has killed two of its own claims, on the record.** `DDD-frame-09` and
   `DDD-measure-08` are retired, retained with the corrections that killed them, IDs never reused.
   §10.1. An unfalsifiable framework does not do that, and the retirements are checkable in the
   graph without the paper's help.

**Where the objection still has purchase, stated rather than defended.** Off the closing region the
framework has no measure, so its claims there rest on explanatory utility and coding reliability
rather than on a quantity. The paper says this in §4.4 and §6.1. A reviewer who thinks that is too
weak a footing for the open region is disagreeing with something the paper concedes rather than
catching something it hides.

## The second objection

*"Governance vocabulary cannot be made precise enough to carry weight."* The paper's answer is that
precision here is **partition discipline**, and §4.1 is where to test it: two partitions over the
same act — what governance supplied, and what the world produced — where escape is *nothing* in one
and *a supply mode* in the other, with an explicit statement that neither reduces to the other. If
that distinction does not hold up under a reviewer's pressure, the vocabulary objection lands, and
§4.1 is the place it lands.

## The review being asked for

Three things, in order of value:

1. **Press the vacuity objection at the four answers above.** Which of them is weakest? Is there a
   fifth answer the paper should be making and is not?
2. **Read §4.1's seam guard and §5.2's retirement paragraph closely.** These are where the paper
   draws its finest distinctions — between two partitions, and between *denying* a retired claim and
   *sidestepping* it. Both are load-bearing and both are new prose.
3. **Check the paper's honesty about status.** Every claim carries the graph's own status word, and
   Appendix A is generated from the graph rather than written. If the paper anywhere reads as
   stronger than its nodes, that is a defect and we want it named.

**Not asked for:** validation of the measure itself — that is the companion note's review, already
had — or judgment on the empirical hypotheses, which are unrun by design and owned by a later study.
