# The primer

An operational manual for running the framework on a real engagement. It teaches five
procedures: file a decision, read an act, test whether a check closes, find the decisions
nobody made, and know where this framework stops. It is not the argument for the framework;
that lives in canon and in Paper A, and §6 says exactly what this manual does not carry.

## 1. What this is for

*Hand-written against `actor-indexed-determination v5.12.0`; the quotation below is generated
from `meta/the-declaration.md`.*

<!-- primer:generated id=declaration -->
> **Who does the work?**
>
> Every organisation has had to answer this, and the instruments for answering it all make the same
> move: where you cannot check the work, check the worker. Grade, seniority, licence, certification
> — each is a standing claim about a supplier, used where the output itself could not be verified
> directly.
>
> That move worked while there was one kind of answerer. There are now two, and the instruments do
> not transfer. A licence is worth something because the licensed person outlives the claims they
> make; model versions do not. A grade is informative because human capability is broadly
> correlated; model capability is not — it is superhuman in one region and incompetent in the one
> beside it. And a credential is issued by an institution that carries stake; a benchmark issues
> nothing and answers for nothing.
>
> The question needs a smaller unit. Work decomposes into decisions. Ask, for each decision, what
> must be determined, whether there is a check that closes, and from where the determination is
> supplied. Every determination can still be supplied by a person; that option never goes away. What
> changes is that the choice is made per decision rather than per job — and each way of supplying a
> determination carries a different cost, a different assurance, and a different answer to who is
> accountable when it is wrong. The framework does not tell you which to choose. It tells you what
> you are choosing between, and it makes the choice one you make on the record.

> What follows is procedural: how to file a decision so it can be found, how to read an act and see
> what actually governed it, how to tell whether a check closes for the arrangement you have, and how
> to find the decisions nobody made. Each procedure states what it does not cover.

One consequence carries the whole manual: every governing decision is supplied from somewhere,
or from nowhere. The work is placement — choosing, per decision and on the record, where each
determination comes from — and escape-prevention: making sure none is supplied by nobody.

## 2. Filing a decision

*Hand-written against `actor-indexed-determination v5.12.0`; term boxes generated at the pin.*

### 2.1 Admit it

<!-- primer:generated id=term:admission-test -->
> **A choice is a decision iff varying *the choice* moves the outcome past tolerance.**
>
> **A fact is ground iff varying *the world* moves the outcome past tolerance.**
>
> — `term:admission-test` (registry status *settled*, established by `00-primitives.md`, at `v5.12.0`)
<!-- /primer:generated -->

Run the test before filing anything. If varying the choice does not move the outcome past
tolerance, it is not a decision and does not file; if varying the world does, what you have is
ground, and it is recorded as ground, not as a decision. The test needs a tolerance to run
against:

<!-- primer:generated id=term:tolerance -->
> **Tolerance** is the declared boundary of acceptable outcome deviation. It indexes
> everything: a choice is a governing decision, and a fact is ground, only relative to a
> declared tolerance. Without one, the decision set is not well-formed.
>
> — `term:tolerance` (registry status *settled*, established by `00-primitives.md`, at `v5.12.0`)
<!-- /primer:generated -->

Declare the tolerance first. A register whose entries carry no tolerance cannot be admitted or
audited, only believed.

### 2.2 Declare where it applies

A decision must declare a resolvable applicability predicate, or explicitly declare itself
universal. There is no third option: a predicate nobody can evaluate is how non-evaluation
silently becomes non-applicability, and a sweep then reads the gap as coverage. Where the
predicate reads declared ground axes, mark each axis as mechanically-evaluable or
judgement-evaluable — the axis-type field — so a reader knows which parts of "does this apply
here?" a machine can answer. This is a filing rule (upstream `DDD-ground-01`, normative); what
following it buys you is a claim, and claims live in §6.

### 2.3 Assign the store

<!-- primer:generated id=term:store -->
> **{rule, check, actor, nothing}.** There is no fifth source.
>
> — `term:store` (registry status *settled*, established by `01-the-principle.md`, at `v5.12.0`)
<!-- /primer:generated -->

Name which of the four supplies this decision at the acts it governs: a rule (standing,
mechanical, before the act), a check (mechanical, at or after the act), an actor (judgement at
the act), or — declared, not defaulted — nothing. Then record timing against the act: before,
during, after. A deliberately-open resolution has no resolution for a timing predicate to
read; its timing value is "—(open)", and any form that forces a definite value misfiles it.

### 2.4 Write a record that can be read later

The invariant core, whatever the register's format: what was determined (the statement, or
`OPEN` with the question), the principal who ruled it, when, what it rests on (its basis
edges), how it is assured, and what would trigger revisiting it. A retro-filed decision
carries two further fields: when the gap was uncovered — distinct from when the act occurred —
and that it was retro-filed. Without those two, retro-filing launders escape into coverage and
a later sweep reports clean.

### 2.5 Two filings, from real registers

**Filing an open question as a decision.** `DDD-dec-14` (this repository) files the
identity-unit question as `OPEN — … deliberately unresolved; owner Emil`, with a conditional
note naming what would transfer it elsewhere. The openness is the content: the register shows
who owns the question and what resolving it waits on, instead of showing nothing.

**Filing an accepted risk.** A product register (pseudonymised) carries a pending decision
reading, in substance: *the undeclared boundaries stay undeclared; exposure accepted until
review*. Nothing was fixed, and that is the point — the acceptance has a principal, a date and
a review trigger, so the risk realising later is a revisited decision, not an incident with no
author.

## 3. Reading an act

*Hand-written against `actor-indexed-determination v5.12.0`; term boxes generated at the pin.*

<!-- primer:generated id=term:act -->
> The **act** — the bounded episode of determination running from its first governing
> decision to an expressed outcome; the unit demand is counted in. Derived, not primitive:
> an act is decisions resolved against ground. Its exact individuation is earned in `09` —
> one verdict, one act, where the measure exists.
>
> — `term:act` (registry status *settled*, established by `00-primitives.md`, at `v5.12.0`)
<!-- /primer:generated -->

To read an act, answer three questions in order: what governed it, what was delivered, what
was the verdict.

### 3.1 What governed it

List the decisions whose applicability predicates hold at this act — evaluated, not assumed
(§2.2). That list is the act's governing set as the register claims it.

### 3.2 What was delivered

<!-- primer:generated id=term:delivery -->
> **Delivery** is how authored governance reaches an act: **act-triggered** — the act triggers
> retrieval, without judgment — or **judgment-mediated** — it reaches the act only if an actor
> recalls it. Delivery is a property of a decision **at an act-site**, never of the decision
> alone: the same decision can be act-triggered at one act-site and judgment-mediated at
> another, and a path from a decision to an act is act-triggered only if **every** edge on it is.
>
> — `term:delivery` (registry status *draft*, established by `13-delivery.md`, at `v5.12.0`)
<!-- /primer:generated -->

For each decision in the governing set, name the channel that carried it to this act-site.
This is a check, not a formality: **filing is not encoding.** A decision with no channel at
this site — nothing act-triggered, no actor who in fact recalled it — was not standing supply
here, whatever the register says. Delivery is per act-site: the same decision can be
act-triggered at one site and judgement-mediated at the next, and a delivery path is
act-triggered only if every edge on it is. (The claims behind this check are `projected`; §6
names them. The check itself needs no claim: either you can name the channel or you cannot.)

Three channels, from real registers: `DDD-dec-02` (papers-before-tool sequencing) has no
mechanical carrier at all — it reaches an act only if a session recalls it, which is what
judgement-mediated means in practice. A Rust CLI's no-unwrap decision (pseudonymised) is
delivered mechanically at every act by a lint gate, the check running *after* the act — the
check store's defining position. And a ledger rule (pseudonymised) making below-tolerance
states unconstructible is delivered *by construction*: refusal at entry, the strongest channel
there is.

### 3.3 What was the verdict — and would you know if the check never ran?

Record the verdict at the act's declared boundary, and which source actually supplied each
determination — which may differ from §3.1's list, and the difference is §5's business.

Where the act involves a check, apply the two-stage pattern: **a check whose pass state is
indistinguishable from its never-run state is not a check.** Ask what a pass looks like and
what never-ran looks like; if they look the same, you have a belief, not a check. The pattern
in the field: the pin advance filed as `DDD-dec-34` (this repository) committed its predicted
firings to a file *before* touching the pin, with the predicting instrument beside it, then
verified the actual firings after — so a silent instrument could not masquerade as a clean
one. Any check you rely on deserves the same shape: an artefact that exists only if the check
ran.

## 4. The floor, operationally

*Hand-written against `actor-indexed-determination v5.12.0`; term boxes generated at the pin.
This section asserts more than the others; each assertion carries its status.*

<!-- primer:generated id=term:floor -->
The "floor" is the portion of a determination's demand that **cannot be moved off the in-the-moment
actor** — the residue that no amount of encoding or checking can amortise, that must be paid, per
run, in judgment.

> **The intrinsic floor is a property of the acceptance predicate, not of the decision.**
>
> — `term:floor` (registry status *settled*, established by `03-the-floor.md`, at `v5.12.0`)
<!-- /primer:generated -->

The definition does the work: the floor is a property of the acceptance predicate, not of the
decision and not of the actor. So the operational question is about your arrangement, asked
per predicate:

<!-- primer:generated id=term:closure -->
**Effective closure, defined.** A predicate is **closed for an arrangement** when the relevant
ground is observable and adequacy can be evaluated within declared resource, latency, and confidence
bounds. **Decidable** is reserved for the formal special case.
>
> — `term:closure` (registry status *settled*, established by `03-the-floor.md`, at `v5.12.0`)
<!-- /primer:generated -->

**Does the predicate close for *this* arrangement, over material it can inspect, within the
bounds you have declared?** Not "is it checkable in principle" — observable ground, evaluable
adequacy, your resource, latency and confidence bounds.

The consequence is a fork with no third prong: **check the work, or check the worker.** Where
the predicate closes, verify outcomes and stop paying for supplier credentials at that
predicate; closure says nothing about how hard the work is to produce — that is a second,
independent variable (`DDD-frame-06`, established). Where it does not close, the demand rides
on the actor: name an accountable supplier and assure the worker, because no instrument will
assure the work.

What this section may assert, by status: closure being distinct from generation cost, and the
verdict-entropy construction being available exactly where the predicate closes for the
arrangement, are `established` (formal — an argued derivation, unchallenged within the
framework; not external validation). The capacity mechanism is `reported`, with its
computation reproducing: capacity-generated escape requires **overflow ∩ open**, both
necessary — demand past capacity *and* no verifier held (`DDD-floor-01`). Carry its scope
correction (upstream `DDD-dec-15`): the intersection is *one generator* of escape, sufficient
and not necessary, never escape's definition. Overflow alone on a closing predicate produces
retries — recoverable, not floor. Open alone within capacity is carried by judgement, where an
accountable supplier is named. The relational reading of the floor — irreducibility as a
property of the whole indexed arrangement — is `projected` and stays in §6.

Both halves of the fork in one real decision: the upstream pin of this repository. The
standing pin is a closed predicate — a checker evaluates it mechanically on every run: check
the work. The decision to *advance* the pin is a governed judgement act with a named principal:
check the worker. One object, two predicates, two correct arrangements.

## 5. Finding escape

*Hand-written against `actor-indexed-determination v5.12.0`; term boxes generated at the pin.*

<!-- primer:generated id=term:escape -->
> **Escaped** — determined *never*, by nobody: decided-by-nobody as a first-class category.
> Latent defect exposure. **The only forbidden state.**
>
> — `term:escape` (registry status *settled*, established by `01-the-principle.md`, at `v5.12.0`)
<!-- /primer:generated -->

Escape is supply-general: however the failure happened, the state it names is a determination
supplied by nobody. You do not find it by looking for dramatic failures; you find it by
sweeping a register against its acts.

### 5.1 Declare the sweep's coordinate system — as part of the sweep

Before sweeping, record: which registers, which population of acts, which axes you will
evaluate, and the date. Record it **beside the result, as part of the deliverable** — not as a
caveat you remember to add. A coverage figure is completeness relative to the declared
coordinate system, and silence outside it is not evidence of coverage. A sweep that does not
record what it swept is the two-stage failure of §3.3 wearing a different coat: its "clean"
is indistinguishable from "never looked".

### 5.2 Sweep the generators

**Capacity.** Where residual demand exceeds what the actor can carry *and* no verifier is
held, the excess escapes:

<!-- primer:generated id=term:escape-mechanism -->
> **Capacity-generated escape — the escape an actor produces from residual it has taken up —
> requires two conditions, both necessary:**
>
> **(1) Overflow** — demand exceeds resolve capacity.
> **(2) Open** — no verifier the actor holds.
>
> Overflow alone (closing predicate) → **retries, not escape.** Recoverable. Not floor.
> Open alone (within capacity) → **carried by judgment**, where an accountable supplier is
> named. Not floor. Where none is named, it is escape by another route (`05` §7) — outside
> this mechanism, not excluded by it.
> **Overflow AND open** → **escape. This is the floor.**
>
> **Sufficient for escape, never necessary.** A governing decision that never entered an
> actor's residual escapes without overflowing anything: escape is supplied-by-nobody
> (`term:escape`), and capacity shortfall is one generator of it.
>
> — `term:escape-mechanism` (registry status *settled*, established by `11-the-floor-mechanism.md`, at `v5.12.0`)
<!-- /primer:generated -->

In a register this looks like an actor assigned acts whose predicates do not close, at a
volume no one has priced.

**Delivery failure.** Run §3.2's channel check register-wide. Filed, adequate, and never
reached the act:

<!-- primer:generated id=term:undelivered -->
> **Undelivered** — filed, adequate, and never reached the act. No source supplied the governing
> decision at the act, so it was determined by nobody: escape, with a distinguishing feature —
> **the ledger shows coverage.** Escape that presents as governance.
>
> — `term:undelivered` (registry status *draft*, established by `13-delivery.md`, at `v5.12.0`)
<!-- /primer:generated -->

This is the generator a healthy-looking register hides best, because the ledger shows
coverage. The canon programme found it in itself: five delivered governing artefacts — a
manuscript, a review, two notes, a gate ruling — did not arrive at their acts, and the
evidence is filed in `DDD-dec-17` and `DDD-dec-20`. The fix was not effort but a channel: the
session-arrival convention made the charter a repo object instead of an artefact recalled from
residue.

**Silent non-applicability.** Predicates that were never evaluated, read as "did not apply"
(§2.2's rule violated). Look for decisions whose applicability no artefact shows being
evaluated against the acts in your population.

**The unfiled decision.** Visible only retroactively — an act whose outcome-relevant
alternative no filed decision governs. Retro-file it with §2.4's two fields, so the repair is
distinguishable from coverage that was always there. A register can also carry its own
tripwires: one production decision (pseudonymised) files a `revisit_if` condition — the
decision's content *is* its revisitation trigger, escape-prevention written as a field.

### 5.3 Read the result

Report escapes found, per generator, against the declared coordinates — and report the
coordinates. One generator is known and not swept by this procedure: the empty-option-set
generator, open and unexamined in canon (§6).
