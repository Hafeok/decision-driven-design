## R-1 — the discharge partition (review §4)

`DDD-frame-15` states four discharge modes: filed decision · judgment · arrangement default ·
uncontrolled draw. The reviewer demonstrates they overlap under the paper's own definitions:

- **declared default vs filed decision** — a declared, governed default is both;
- **deterministic rule vs judgment** — a thermostat reads ground at the act and produces output by a
  rule fixed beforehand, which canon times as *encoded* while the gloss of judgment ("variation
  produced at the act by an actor reading ground") reads as covering it;
- **trained inference** — policy-level commitment (Paper A §3.3), judgment (§4.1), and standing
  supply (§10.3, `DDD-cost-20`) at once.

**Two dispositions, both drafted, Emil rules:**
- **(a) a priority rule** assigning each case exactly once, or
- **(b) the modes recast as orthogonal axes** (the reviewer names the mixed dimensions: causal
  producer, provenance, time of resolution, governance status, control, delivery).

**The proposed rule or axes must be tested against six cases before it is proposed, not after:**
trained inference · lookup tables · declared defaults · randomised search with checking ·
abstention · timeout. A disposition that cannot classify all six exactly once is not ready.

**One question the review does not ask and the session should:** the discharge modes borrow store
vocabulary — *filed decision* and *judgment* are also store names — while partitioning a different
object. Does the confusion come partly from the shared words? Report whether distinct names for the
discharge modes would carry more of the repair than a priority rule does.

**Untouched:** the seam guard. It separates governance-supply from discharge, is correctly ratified,
and is orthogonal to these overlaps. **Record for the manifest:** an external reader met the guard
and still reported "determined never, by nobody" as conflicting — a finding about the guard's
audience, not its content.

## R-2 — residual discretion (review §5)

Separate the four; then rule whether any of them mints. **Mint after the separation, never before.**
