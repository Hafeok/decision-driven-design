# F-1 — Ruling 16: are the B1 reductions one account or several?

## The booking is two; the source says three

The session manifest books this as a live pair — shared-budget (§7.3) against membership (§13.7).
**The note books three.** §13.7's own closing flag:

> *Flagged, structural:* this is the **third** construct this session that reduces B1 (shared
> budget, §7.3; membership, here; and the disposition framing, §13.1). Each felt like progress. The
> corpus test must check whether these are one account or three; if three, the model carries
> unnoticed redundancy.

and §13.11 lists among the open rulings *"whether the three B1 reductions are one account (ruling
16)"*. Emil's GATE 4 ruling: the source governs over the booking, present all three, and the third
gets its disposal stated rather than assumed.

## The three constructs

| | Construct | Where | What it makes escape turn on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Shared budget** | §7.3 | Execution capacity per actor pays for doing, retrieving *and* authoring. Escape where the budget runs out — including where residual is well inside what could have been resolved, because the budget went to retrieval. |
| **2** | **Membership** | §13.7 | Capability is *a set of trained decisions ready for fast retrieval*. An unfilled link facing an actor whose capability contains a matching resolution is **judged**; facing one whose capability does not, it **escapes**. |
| **3** | **Disposition framing** | §13.1 | Judgement and escape are *"not two stores sharing a bucket … two dispositions of unencoded demand, separated by actor capability."* |

## Construct 3 — self-disposed by the note, and the disposal needs confirming

§13.1 carries its own revision-9 annotation:

> *(Revision 9: this was written when B1 was booked as blocked; the capacity model is in fact
> ratified in live canon. The disposition framing is therefore not an anticipation of a pending
> model but a redescription of a landed one, and must be read against `core/11`'s actual machinery
> — where it adds nothing over `term:capacity` and `term:escape-mechanism`, it is redundant. Ruling
> 16 as reframed carries the question.)*

Verified against live canon at head: `term:capacity` (settled, `core/11` §2), `term:overflow`
(settled), `term:escape-mechanism` (settled, and now carrying F-3's scope note), `DDD-floor-01`
(reported). The construct's content — *two dispositions of unencoded demand separated by actor
capability* — is `term:escape-mechanism`'s two conjuncts with different words. It proposes no
observation the mechanism does not already make.

**Disposal, for confirmation:** construct 3 is redundant against landed canon and is not a third
account. It leaves ruling 16 as the two the manifest booked. *A disposal is a ruling; without one,
§13.1's construct stays formally undisposed, which is the species of loose end this session exists
to close.*

## The evidence, on constructs 1 and 2

**(a) Corpus question-3 — the discriminating test that returned no discrimination.**

> Across all 11 expressions and all 7 examined gates, **no classification ever invoked a
> magnitude**: every store, delivery, and failure reading was carried by presence/arrival objects —
> is a resolution filed, did it arrive, is there anything to deliver (row 11's nothing-to-deliver is
> §13.7's internal location expressed as the absence of a delivery failure mode). The budget was
> never needed. Evidence: on this corpus the membership-style account carried everything the
> expressions required; **nothing here distinguishes the two accounts *for each other*, but nothing
> needed the second.**

Read precisely, this is an **absence of need**, not evidence of reduction. The corpus was expression
work over a decision register; magnitude was never invoked because nothing in that corpus was under
load. It is silence, and the note's own discipline forbids reading silence as demonstration
(`DDD-floor-01`: *"an absence of counter-instances, not a demonstration"*).

**(b) The b1-reconciliation note (upstream `meta/`) — canon separates two accounts, ratified.**

> **Canon now separates two accounts of escape**, and the separation is ratified rather than
> conjectural:
> - a **capacity-shaped** account — demand an actor took up, exceeded its budget, and shed with no
>   verifier to catch it;
> - a **membership-shaped** account — a governing decision no supplier took up at all, escaped
>   because `term:escape` is stated over supply generally and `term:store` admits no fifth source.

with its instruction to the reading session:

> **Report the instances. Do not rule on the accounts.** `DDD-dec-15` settled the scope; it did not
> settle which account is primary, and this session has no standing to.

**This evidence is directional and it matters.** `DDD-dec-15` establishes that the membership shape
covers five ratified instances the capacity shape does not — the capacity account is *sufficient for
escape, never necessary*. That is a one-way result: membership reaches cases capacity cannot. It is
**not** a reduction of capacity to membership, and `DDD-floor-01`'s falsifier limb 2 (escape on
decisions with a held verifier) did not fire.

**(c) §13.7's own argument for membership as the operable object.**

> This is a better-behaved object than capacity-as-budget: it has extension (you can ask which
> decisions are in it), it is typed, and it maps onto the graph rather than sitting beside it. It is
> part of why B1 kept resisting — **the budget was a scalar where the object is a set.**

and, decisively for the question actually asked:

> **Both may be needed (a capable actor can still be overloaded), but membership is the operable
> one.**

The note's own author does not claim reduction. It claims **operability**.

**(d) The delivery claims** (`DDD-delivery-01/02/03`, `term:delivery`, `term:undelivered`,
`term:presumed-discharge`, pinned). These sit on the membership side by construction: an
externalised resolution has a delivery failure mode, an internalised one has none —

> Internalised resolutions have **no delivery failure mode** — nothing to deliver — which is why
> they are the strong mechanism and the dangerous one.

Delivery vocabulary discriminates *arrival*, not *magnitude*. It supplies no test between the
accounts; it is further membership-side machinery.

## The two readings, drafted

### Reading A — one account. Membership is primary; the budget is its scalar summary

**The claim.** Capability is a set of resolutions; capacity-as-budget is a lossy scalar over that
set. Escape occurs where the required resolution is not retrievable by the arrangement at the act.
Overflow is then not a second mechanism but a **membership failure under load**: the set is large
enough in principle, and the arrangement cannot reach enough of it per act, so the resolutions it
fails to reach are not, at that act, members of what it can retrieve. One object, two views.

**What would file.** An amendment to `DDD-cost-05`'s region declaring the capacity term a derived
reading of a membership account; the capacity model retained as a limiting case with its assets
intact; `term:capacity` unamended but glossed as a summary statistic.

**What is right about it.** It explains why B1 resisted: a scalar was standing in for a set. It
explains the corpus silence economically — no magnitude was invoked because magnitude is never the
primary question. And it makes the account *checkable*, which §13.7 correctly identifies as the
budget's weakness.

**What is wrong with it, or at least unpaid.** Nothing in the evidence establishes the reduction.
The corpus returned silence, not discrimination. `DDD-floor-01`'s mechanism is `reported` on two
reproducing assets that model shedding under load, and no asset or instance shows those results
recovered from a membership account. Filing this reading would repeat the exact error `DDD-dec-15`
corrected — asserting a universal on an absence of counter-instances — and it would do so in the
same claim region.

### Reading B — two accounts. Distinct mechanisms; membership is operable, capacity is real

**The claim.** Capacity-generated escape and membership escape are two generators of one category.
`term:escape` indexes on supply alone and `term:store` admits no fifth source, so both are escape;
neither reduces to the other. Canon has already ruled they come apart in one direction
(`DDD-dec-15`, five instances). The corpus establishes that membership is the operable account for
register work; that is a statement about instrumentation, not about ontology.

**What would file.** Nothing new, or almost nothing: a note on `DDD-cost-05` and
`term:escape-mechanism` recording that the two accounts are non-reducible pending evidence, with
ruling 16 closed as *two accounts, membership operable, capacity real, reduction unevidenced*.

**What is right about it.** It matches every piece of evidence in hand and asserts nothing beyond
them. It preserves §13.7's own hedge verbatim — *both may be needed; a capable actor can still be
overloaded*. It leaves the reduction hypothesis alive as a question rather than killing or crowning
it.

**What is wrong with it.** It is unambitious, and it leaves the redundancy §13.7 worried about
("if three, the model carries unnoticed redundancy") only partly resolved — construct 3 is disposed,
constructs 1 and 2 stand side by side with no test between them. It books a debt rather than paying
one.

## What the session recommends, and what it will not do

The evidence supports **Reading B**, and the recommendation is to close ruling 16 as: *construct 3
redundant and disposed; constructs 1 and 2 are two accounts, not one; membership is the operable
account on all evidence to date; the reduction in Reading A is unevidenced and stays open.*

The session will **not** file Reading A on this evidence. Doing so would assert a universal from an
absence, in the same region where `DDD-dec-15` corrected exactly that error four days earlier, and
against the b1-reconciliation note's explicit instruction not to rule on the accounts from
instance-reporting alone. **Emil rules; filing is whatever the ruling licenses and no more.**

# F-2 — Does `C_resolve` accounting extend to retrieval and authoring draws?

## The question, from the source

§7.3, in full on the point:

> Emil's formulation: execution capacity per actor is what pays for doing, retrieving, or making
> decisions.
>
> **Consequence for the blocked work.** The actor-capacity model was booked as needed to cleave
> judgement from escape in `H(V|X)` … If retrieval and authoring draw on the same budget, capacity
> is consumed by finding governance and by deciding at all, not only by resolving. **Escape can then
> occur with residual well inside what the actor could have resolved, because the budget went to
> retrieval.** *(Revision 9: the model is ratified, not blocked … it is now a proposed **extension
> to a landed model** — whether `C_resolve` accounting should include retrieval and authoring draws
> — which is a canon amendment question for after the corpus test, not pre-work for a pending
> model.)*

**The precondition is met.** The revision-9 annotation defers this to "after the corpus test"; the
corpus test ran on 2026-08-14 and its results are at `meta/corpus-test-results-2026-08-14.md`. The
question is properly before Emil now.

## The two constraints, held

**(1) Commensurability is unestablished.**

> One budget assumes a common unit, and the framework has a unit for one draw only — `H(V)`, on
> closing predicates. Retrieval cost is search over an index; authoring cost is prior commitment.
> **One budget versus three trading at a rate is a modelling claim with the same status as the
> demand identification, and should be marked so rather than adopted as structure.**

This is exact and it is the harder constraint. `DDD-measure-11` is already canon on the adjacent
point: the measure prices the **verdict**, not the **search** — *"two tasks with identical verdict
entropy can differ unboundedly in generation cost."* Retrieval is search. So canon has already
declared that one of the three draws is outside the unit `C_resolve` is denominated in.

**(2) Per arrangement, not per actor.**

> The same model with retrieval and tests is a different capacity from the same model without.
> *Execution capacity per actor* should read *per arrangement*, or it reintroduces the species
> framing removed upstream.

§13.7 repeats it independently — *"capability is **per arrangement**, not per actor — the
resolutions may live in the tooling rather than the weights"* — and §7.4 sharpens it for the LLM
case: *"Weights and forward pass are stateless … The **arrangement** generally is not — this session
had memory, tools, and a file. Same weights, different capacity."*

## A finding the booking did not anticipate

**Constraint (2) and item F-3 are the same defect.** F-3 files a scope note on
`term:escape-mechanism` because the open conjunct is actor-indexed — *"no verifier **the actor
holds**"* — and is ill-defined where no actor was assigned. Constraint (2) says capacity is
actor-indexed where the object is per-arrangement. Both conjuncts of the escape mechanism are
indexed to an actor, and the note argues on independent grounds that the correct index is the
arrangement.

`term:arrangement` is settled canon and says so already: *"The unit of comparison is the
arrangement, not the isolated actor."* `DDD-cost-05` nonetheless reads *"Capacity is the bits an
**actor** can supply per act."*

This is recorded, not filed. It is a third question adjacent to F-2 — *should the capacity model be
re-indexed from actor to arrangement?* — and it is larger than either F item's booking. Naming it is
this session's job; answering it is not.

## The two readings, drafted

### Reading A — extend `C_resolve` to cover retrieval and authoring draws

**The claim.** Execution capacity per **arrangement** is one budget paying for retrieving
governance, authoring resolutions, and resolving. Escape can occur with residual inside what the
arrangement could have resolved, because the budget went elsewhere. `DDD-cost-05`'s region widens
accordingly.

**Evidence for it.** §7.4's LLM case is the strongest instance available: *"Context is
simultaneously the ground, the retrieved governance, and the working space for resolving — three
competing draws sharing one store with no accounting separating them. For this arrangement type the
three uses are literally one budget, a cleaner case than can be made for a human."* Note that §7.4 is
**parked by Emil**, so this evidence sits behind a hold.

**What would file.** `DDD-cost-05`'s statement and region amended; the three draws named; the
commensurability assumption marked as a modelling claim in the claim's own `region:`; a falsifier
naming the observation that would break it (an arrangement whose retrieval draw demonstrably does
not trade against its resolve draw).

**What is wrong with it.** It adopts as structure precisely what constraint (1) says must be marked
as a claim. And it runs against live canon: `DDD-measure-11` holds that the measure does not price
search, so folding search into a budget denominated in that measure needs an exchange rate nobody
has. Filing it would also silently answer the actor-versus-arrangement question above, in passing,
without ruling on it.

### Reading B — do not extend; record the question with its two constraints

**The claim.** `C_resolve` stays denominated in the unit it has — bits of verdict on closing
predicates. The proposition that retrieval and authoring draw on the same budget is recorded on
`DDD-cost-05` as a **named open extension** with both constraints attached, and is not adopted.

**What would file.** One paragraph in `DDD-cost-05`'s `notes:`, naming the extension, both
constraints, the §7.4 hold, and the arrangement-indexing question — enough that the next session
inherits the question fully formed rather than re-deriving it. No statement, region, status,
evidence or falsifier moves.

**What is right about it.** It honours constraint (1) exactly as the source demands: *marked so
rather than adopted as structure*. It does not pre-empt the actor/arrangement ruling. It costs one
paragraph and closes the freight item at its booked size.

**What is wrong with it.** It is a deferral wearing a filing's clothes. If the extension is correct
— and the LLM case is genuinely compelling — then canon carries a capacity model known to be
narrower than the phenomenon for however long the question stays open.

## What the session recommends

**Reading B.** Constraint (1) is not a caution, it is a blocker: extending a budget across three
draws requires a common unit, canon explicitly denies having one for search (`DDD-measure-11`), and
the source itself says the one-budget form *"should be marked so rather than adopted as structure"*.
Reading A cannot be filed without either inventing the exchange rate or leaving it unstated — and
leaving it unstated is the failure mode this repository's own rules exist to prevent.

Emil rules. Filing is whatever the ruling licenses and no more.

# F-3 — the actor-indexed `open` conjunct: DONE

Filed, not ruled — the item books a scope note, and the scope note is what landed.

`core/graph/terms.yaml`, on `term:escape-mechanism`, records that the open conjunct is
actor-indexed and therefore **ill-defined, not merely false**, where no actor was assigned; that on
the five ratified no-supplier instances there is no actor whose verifier holdings can be asked
about, so the conjunct does not evaluate; that this bounds where the mechanism can be **asked**,

taken the residual up by construction, so the conjunct is well-formed there. It carries the
b1-reconciliation note's instruction verbatim in substance: *a specimen that will not classify is a
datum, not a coding failure — record it as undefined rather than forcing it.*

**Filed as a registry comment, not as canonical text — a choice, and reversible.** The note sits on
the registry entry above `canonical_md`, so the term's ratified canonical text is untouched and no
re-projection into `core/11` §3 is needed. The alternative — folding the limit into `canonical_md`
— would supersede settled text that `DDD-dec-15` re-scoped four days ago, to record a limit the
same decision explicitly carried forward rather than repaired. Given "supersession, never rewriting"
and a booking that says *file the scope note*, the lighter instrument is the right one.

**Ruling sought:** confirm the registry-comment placement, or promote the note into `canonical_md`
with the supersession and re-projection that requires.
