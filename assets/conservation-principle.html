The Law of Conservation of Specification Demand

For a task at a declared assurance level, specification demand is fixed by the task — never by the system you build to handle it. Every governing decision gets made. The only choice is by whom, when, and at what price.

Encoded and Mechanical are the same specification at different binding times — constraint before the act, criterion after. Redundancy is permitted. Only uncovered-and-unpriced is forbidden.

All three boundaries move, and the total never does. Drag the right-hand boundary outward to pull demand out of escape and into judgment — and watch it hit the actor's capacity and stop. That wall is the floor. Beyond it, the only handle that helps is the one on the far left: encode it. Escape is never something you choose. It is what the other three fail to absorb.

Actors — only the judgment store is carried by one

An actor is anything that makes decisions. They differ in how tightly you can pin them — and in how much judgment they can absorb before demand starts escaping.

If the governing decisions and the acceptance predicate both close over digital ground, the intrinsic floor is zero. Which means: in digital work, "you need a good senior for this" never describes an intrinsic floor. It describes an unpaid transfer floor.

The consequence
Withheld specification relocates demand onto the actor's floor.

This is the tier–specification inverse law, and it settles an old confusion. Special operations runs at an extreme tier — brutal selection, constant training, tiny teams — because its ground is adversarial and shifting and genuinely will not hold encoding. The encoded store is capped by the domain. Demand is conserved. It lands in judgment. Selection is floor measurement; they screen for the component they cannot manufacture.

The senior-dependent consultancy adopted the shape and skipped the justification. But software runs on digital ground, where the intrinsic floor is zero. It was paying spec-ops prices for a floor that was never intrinsic — only unencoded.

In spec ops the tier is high because the specification cannot be encoded. In consulting it was high because it wasn't. Same posture, opposite verdict — and only one of them survives an actor you can pin by binding.
