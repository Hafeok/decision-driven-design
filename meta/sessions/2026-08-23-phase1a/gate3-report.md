## 1. The naming check — two of three collided, and the structure survives

You ordered this as naming, not structure, and it stayed that way. But both collisions were real,
and one was in the seam-critical direction.

### `fixed` — collides, and in the one place that matters

`term:encoded` reads:

> **Encoded** — a constraint, fixed *before* the act, **by a rule**.

**An incidental default takes the discharge-axis value with no rule authored and no governance
supplied.** It is determined by configuration and ground; it is not *encoded* in the store sense at
all. The shared word would have read the discharge axis back into the store partition — **the one
thing this claim exists to prevent.** Governance status would have re-entered by lexical back door
after being excluded by construction.

`term:commitment-level` compounds it, using "fixed" twice for the **composing** axis's own content:
*"permitted resolutions **fixed** directly"*, *"the generating procedure **fixed**"*. Axis 1 and
Axis 2 would have shared their most load-bearing word.

### `resolved` — collides worse, and as a genus rather than a neighbour

> **Determination** is the **resolving** of a decision against ground… **Nothing else resolves
> anything.**

Also `term:actor` (*"resolves decisions by reading ground"*), `term:capacity` (*"resolve capacity"*,
*"jointly resolved"*), and `term:pinning-resolution`. **Every value on this axis is a resolving.**
Naming one of three after the genus is a category error visible from the registry alone, without
any reader's confusion to prompt it.

### `drawn` — clean, and kept

Ten occurrences in `core/`, none definitional. Its only technical neighbour is `core/09`'s *"drawn
at each act from a fixed eight-outcome prior"* — the intended sense exactly — and it is continuous
with the luck ruling's corrected wording, as you said.

### The values are now `predetermined` · `exercised` · `drawn`

> …each outcome-relevant alternative is discharged in exactly one of three ways: **predetermined**
> — the arrangement's standing configuration together with the ground at the act determines the
> resolution; **exercised** — it does not, and something within the arrangement's control
> determines it at the act; or **drawn** — it does not, and what determines it lies outside the
> arrangement's control.

`predetermined` occurs **nowhere in `core/`** and says what the value means without importing "by a
rule". `exercised` has no definitional use in the registry, and it joins the axis to
`term:residual-discretion`'s own sentence — *"a cryptographic hash… **exercises** no discretion"* —
which makes the convergence lexical rather than merely argued.

**One wobble recorded rather than smoothed.** A seeded generator inside the arrangement is not
obviously *exercising* anything. The answer is that the value names **what the arrangement does
with an alternative its commitments left open**, not what the immediate producer is — which is the
same property that keeps actorhood off this axis. The oddity and the virtue are one thing. If you
want it changed, the structure is indifferent.

**Rejected, with reasons:** `settled` (it is a **term status value** in the registry), `selected`
(`term:selection` means something else entirely — verification relocated onto the actor's
identity), `carried` (`terms.yaml` already reads *"carried by judgment"*, store-side allocation
vocabulary), `occasioned` (`DDD-frame-16`'s word, and `DDD-frame-16` is a composing axis).

things, of which **only the first came from outside**:

### `DDD-measure-16` — `formal`, **`established`**

> The verdict-entropy construction is available to an arrangement exactly where the task's
> acceptance predicate closes for that arrangement; availability is a property of the arrangement,
> not of the task, and it is neither the existence of the verdict function nor the estimability of
> the ground distribution.

Existence and estimability sit in `region:`, which is the field the schema provides for boundedness
— *"Region is mandatory. 'Everywhere' must be written to be claimed."* `established` survives
because availability-given-closure is definitional on the measure side and rule 4 holds that an
identity that holds is `formal` and can be established.

**Falsifier**, per your ruling that there is no near-definitional exception:

> An arrangement for which the acceptance predicate closes and the verdict function exists, and for
> which the construction is nevertheless unavailable for a reason that is neither an estimability
> failure nor a failure of closure; or an arrangement that computes `H(V)` over a predicate that
> does not close for it.

The predecessor's `test:` carries forward and is **sharper in its new home**: a task with an open
predicate for which a defensible verdict function exists anyway now falsifies nothing about
availability and falsifies the region's *existence* condition, which is the question it was always
asking.

### `DDD-measure-17` — `conceptual`, **`projected`**

> The construction's domain and the region where the framework's floor result locates non-zero
> floor coincide, and the coincidence is principled rather than evidential: the two arguments share
> the closure of the acceptance predicate as a premise, so their agreement about where the line
> falls is close to definitional on the measure's side and is not evidence that the identification
> is correct.

`kind: conceptual` deliberately — what is asserted is a relation between two arguments and their
premises, not an identity that holds. `DDD-measure-16` is the formal half. Rule 4 forbids fusing
them, which is what the predecessor did.

**Its falsifier's first limb is the interesting one**, and it is why the demotion is honest rather
than merely cautious:

> A demonstration that the two arguments do not in fact share the closure premise — that the floor
> result can be derived without it, or that the construction's domain can be stated without it — in
> which case the agreement would be substantive and this claim would understate it; **or** an
> arrangement for which the two lines demonstrably part…

**The claim can fail by understating as well as by overstating.** A demotion that could only fail
in one direction would be a hedge.

### What the demotion does not remove

The line was drawn twice, from different materials, with neither fitted to the other. That remains
true and it is still the measure note's best feature. What goes is **the inference from the
agreement to the correctness of the identification** — an inference the node never had warrant for,
and which its own evidence field told it not to make.
